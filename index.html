The Erosion of Trust: "Big Government's Spectacular Fails"

During the 1940s and 1950s, Republicans and Democrats actually looked very similar in many of their ideals. Both parties made fighting the Cold War their top foreign policy priority, and both favored a significant role for the federal government in domestic affairs.

Stop 2 of 5 • Population & Cultural Shifts

Demographic Shifts & "The Me Decade"

"The Me Decade"

Many observers called the 1970s "The Me Decade" because Americans turned inward toward self-improvement and personal vitality rather than collective social crusades:

  • Rise of Transcendental Meditation & Eastern relaxation practices.
  • Boom in personal fitness: the NY Marathon grew from ~100 men in 1970 to 14,000 in 1980.
  • Bodybuilding & muscular action stars like Sylvester Stallone and Arnold Schwarzenegger rose in Hollywood.

Movement to the Suburbs

Population trends (demographics) reshaped American politics in favor of conservatism:

  • Traditionally strong liberal northern cities lost population and political clout as families moved out to the suburbs.
  • Suburban homeowners faced financial struggles (inflation/property taxes) and became receptive to targeted conservative messaging against high taxes and urban spending.

Rust Belt to Sunbelt

Millions of white Americans migrated from the industrial Rust Belt (Northeast/Midwest) to the growing Sunbelt (South/Southwest):

  • Since the Civil War, most white voters in the Deep South voted Democratic ("Solid South").
  • Combined with backlash to the Civil Rights Act of 1964, this migration fueled a massive rise of the Republican Party across the South.

Stop 3 of 5 • Economic & Social Grievances

Conservative Backlash Against Liberal Policies & Court Rulings

Click through each of the 6 major domestic flashpoints below to see why conservatives mobilized against liberal programs and Supreme Court decisions:

1. High Taxes

Tax Revolt Sentiment

Conservatives argued federal taxes had grown far too high to fund expanding government programs, hurting working families and stifling business investment.

2. Great Society Programs

Critique of Welfare

Conservatives argued Great Society programs made poverty worse, imposed unfunded requirements on states, rewarded a lack of effort, and contributed to an increase in babies born out of wedlock—weakening the traditional family.

3. Affirmative Action

"Reverse Discrimination"

Policies designed to increase minority representation in hiring and college admissions were criticized by conservatives as unfair "reverse discrimination."

4. Engel v. Vitale

Religion in Public Schools

The Supreme Court decision banning school-sponsored prayer and religious teaching in public schools sparked outrage among religious conservatives.

5. Roe v. Wade

Abortion & Traditional Family

The Supreme Court ruling legalizing abortion became a central rallying point for social conservatives who viewed it as a sign of moral decline and erosion of the traditional family.

6. Equal Rights Amendment (ERA)

Debate Over Gender Roles

Opponents warned the ERA would force women into male combat roles, repeal alimony and child custody protections, and divide women (middle-class vs. poor minority women; housewives vs. working women).

Stop 4 of 5 • Political Organization

Building the "New Right" Coalition

The New Right grew rapidly as a conglomerate of distinct groups—from mainstream religious and business leaders to radical fringe elements—united against liberal policies.

Pillar 1: The Religious Right

Televangelists & Voter Mobilization

Religious leaders used television and grassroots organizing to combat what they viewed as America's moral decline (e.g., Roe v. Wade):

  • Pat Robertson: Founded the Christian Broadcast Network (CBN) and hosted The 700 Club.
  • Jim Bakker: Formed the Praise the Lord (PTL) Network, reaching huge TV audiences.
  • Jerry Falwell: Founded the Moral Majority, registering over 2 million new voters (almost all voting Republican) using congressional "Report Cards" that graded politicians on moral issues.
Pillar 2: Business Leaders

Free Market & Deregulation

Alongside the religious right, conservative business leaders funded think tanks and campaigns pushing free-market economics:

  • Argued that deficit spending and welfare needed to be cut to reduce the tax burden on families and businesses.
  • Contended that heavy government regulations were stifling economic growth, innovation, and personal wealth creation.
Pillar 3: Fringe Extremists

Radical & Anti-Government Groups

Like many broad political movements, the New Right also had extreme fringe elements on its margins:

  • Racial Hatred Groups: Groups like the Ku Klux Klan joined the outcry against perceived moral/social decline.
  • Ultra-Libertarian Militias: Armed groups like the Michigan Militia formed to oppose a federal government they viewed as invasive.
  • Historical Foreshadowing: Extremist anti-government standoffs would later erupt in the 1990s at Ruby Ridge, Waco, and the Oklahoma City Bombing.

Stop 5 of 5 • Turning Point Election

The Election of 1980 & The Reagan Revolution

Democrat (Incumbent) 1977–1981

President Jimmy Carter

Entered the 1980 campaign weakened and looking like a "lame duck" due to three major crises:

  • Rising Inflation & economic stagnation at home
  • Iran Hostage Crisis (52 Americans held for 444 days)
  • Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan (Cold War tensions)
Republican Challenger Former CA Governor

Ronald Reagan

Former Hollywood actor and Governor of California. In the 1950s, he switched from a hardcore New Deal Democrat to a Republican because he hated the Great Society.

The Pivotal Debate (1 Week Before Election): Relaxed and composed, Reagan eased fears of his inexperience and asked voters: "Are you better off than you were 4 years ago?"

Reagan's 4 Key Conservative Values in 1980

1. Limited Government

Emphasize individual liberty and reduce the size and harmful interference of federal government.

2. Economic Freedom

Free markets, lower taxes, and deregulation to spur individual initiative and growth.

3. Traditional Values

Uphold traditional family structures, morality, and religious beliefs against rapid social change.

4. Strong Defense

A strong military and hawkish foreign policy to maintain global stability and counter the USSR.

1980 Landslide & 1981 Inauguration
"Government is NOT the solution to our problem; government is the problem."

Reagan won 50.5% of the popular vote and nearly 500 electoral votes, while Republicans took control of the U.S. Senate for the first time since 1955. His 1981 agenda: cut taxes, cut government spending, and balance the federal budget.
